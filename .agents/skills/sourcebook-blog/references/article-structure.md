# Article structure guide

Use this when drafting an article or making a substantial structural edit. Identify the intended reader and the one question or task the article should resolve. Keep the author's existing angle when improving a draft.

Choose the shape that fits the material:

- **Explainer:** State the idea and why it matters, build the explanation from a concrete example, then cover limits or tradeoffs.
- **Walkthrough:** State the expected result and prerequisites, show a reproducible sequence, explain what each important step does, then show how to check the result.
- **Release or project update:** Lead with what changed and who is affected. Separate shipped behavior from plans, include migration or compatibility details when they matter, and link to the authoritative release or source.
- **Comparison or opinion:** State the decision being examined, use the same criteria for each option, distinguish evidence from judgment, and say when the conclusion would change.

Open with the reader's problem or payoff. Give each section a distinct job; remove repeated setup and unsupported detours. End with the practical conclusion or next action the article has earned, rather than a generic recap. A short article may need no headings beyond its title.
